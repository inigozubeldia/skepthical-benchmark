You are reviewing the attached scientific paper as a journal referee.
Write a referee report.

As well as your usual referee judgement, check the paper explicitly for
each of these four kinds of error. Give each one a deliberate pass,
rather than noting it only if it happens to catch your eye.

  1. Citation errors. Claims that the works they cite do not actually
     support: a result or method attributed to the wrong work, a claim
     stated more strongly or more specifically than the cited work
     warrants, or a reference that does not bear on the claim at all.

  2. Numerical errors. Numbers that are wrong, or inconsistent with
     other numbers in the paper. Redo the arithmetic wherever the paper
     prints enough for you to check it, and check that the same quantity
     is reported consistently everywhere it appears. Work from the
     numbers printed in the paper rather than from external sources.

  3. Mathematical errors. Equations, derivations and definitions that
     are incorrect: a step that does not follow from the one before it,
     an equation the paper states that is simply wrong, a symbol used
     inconsistently with how it was defined, or units and dimensions
     that do not balance.

  4. General consistency errors. Places where the paper contradicts
     itself, or reaches past its evidence: two parts of the paper making
     incompatible statements about what was done or what was found,
     conclusions stated more broadly than the results demonstrate, or
     claims presented as established without support in the paper.

For each issue you report, say specifically what is wrong, cite where it
occurs (section, equation, table or figure), say why it matters, and give
a concrete fix. List every occurrence of a recurring issue, not just the
first one you find.

Label each issue by severity:
  - major — changes how the results or conclusions should be read;
  - minor — worth fixing for clarity or rigour, but the conclusions
    stand;
  - very minor — typographical, notational or cosmetic.

Report only what the paper itself supports. Do not invent content,
references, equations or numbers. If you suspect a problem but cannot
confirm it, report it and say what you were unable to check — an
explicitly uncertain flag is more useful than a confident guess, and a
confident flag on something that turns out to be correct is worse than
no flag at all. Do not recommend acceptance, revision or rejection.

Return plain markdown under exactly these headings, in this order:

  # Referee report: *<paper title>*
  ## Summary
  ## Strengths
  ## Major issues
  ## Minor issues
  ## Very minor issues

Number the issues under each of the three issue headings, and tag each
one [citation], [numerical], [mathematical] or [general]. Where a
heading has no items, write "_No … reported._" and move on. No JSON, no
code fences.

Write the review now.
